## 1. Purpose

This license is intended to let individuals, independent developers, small teams, small companies, cooperatives, nonprofits, educational projects, and early-stage businesses with limited financial resources inspect, run, modify, test, and develop the Software without obtaining a paid commercial license while they remain within the applicable Community limits.

## 2. Gross Income

For an individual acting on their own behalf, **“Gross Income”** means the total amount of income, compensation, receipts, or other economic benefit received or earned by that individual during the applicable measurement period **before** deductions, expenses, taxes, losses, credits, offsets, or other reductions.

Gross Income includes, without limitation, wages, salaries, tips, bonuses, commissions, contract income, self-employment income, business distributions, investment income, rental income, royalties, platform income, consulting income, and other monetary compensation.

Gross Income is not net income, profit, taxable income, adjusted gross income, or money remaining after expenses.

## 3. Gross Revenue

For a company, organization, partnership, cooperative, nonprofit, startup, project, or other entity, **“Gross Revenue”** means all money or other consideration received or earned by the entity during the applicable measurement period before deducting expenses, taxes, wages, contractor payments, payment-processing fees, infrastructure costs, depreciation, losses, or other deductions, except bona fide customer refunds actually returned to customers.

Gross Revenue includes, without limitation, fares, ride charges, subscriptions, commissions, transaction fees, service fees, platform fees, membership fees, advertising revenue, API charges, licensing revenue, sponsorship revenue, and other receipts.

## 4. Software-Attributable Gross Revenue

**“Software-Attributable Gross Revenue”** means Gross Revenue generated directly or indirectly through use of the Software, including revenue from a product or service in which the Software provides material functionality.

## 5. Eligible Community Developers

A person or entity qualifies as a **“Community Developer”** only while it remains below every applicable financial and usage threshold published by the Licensor for the applicable release or licensing period.

The Community limits may include:

- individual annual Gross Income;
- entity annual Gross Revenue;
- annual Software-Attributable Gross Revenue;
- active users;
- registered users;
- drivers;
- vehicles;
- completed rides;
- transactions;
- locations;
- deployments; or
- another objectively measurable usage limit.

**Current numerical thresholds must be published by the Licensor before they are used to determine that a Community Developer has exceeded a limit.** Until a numerical threshold is published for a particular category, that unpublished category does not independently disqualify a Licensee.

## 6. Individual vs. Entity Eligibility

An individual acting independently is evaluated using the applicable individual Gross Income and usage limits.

A business, company, partnership, nonprofit, cooperative, startup, or other legal entity is evaluated primarily using the entity's Gross Revenue, Software-Attributable Gross Revenue, and applicable usage limits rather than the personal income of its employees, contractors, officers, or developers.

## 7. Permitted Use

While eligible, a Community Developer may:

- inspect the source code;
- copy the Software;
- run the Software in unmodified form;
- modify the Software;
- run modified versions;
- create derivative works;
- use the Software for development, testing, evaluation, education, research, or community projects;
- operate a qualifying small-scale production or Hosted Service; and
- engage in qualifying limited commercial activity while remaining within all applicable Community limits.

## 8. Network Source Requirement

If a Community Developer modifies the Software and permits users to interact with the modified Software remotely through a computer network, the operator must prominently provide those users access to the corresponding source code of the version being operated, including the operator's modifications necessary to build and operate that version.

This requirement applies whether the service is free or revenue-producing.

## 9. Transition to Commercial License

When a Community Developer exceeds any applicable published threshold, previously authorized activity does not become a violation solely because the threshold was later exceeded.

However, continued commercial, production, hosted, SaaS, marketplace, dispatch, rideshare, transportation, delivery, fleet-management, or Competing Service operation after exceeding an applicable threshold requires authorization under `COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.md` or a separate written commercial agreement from the Licensor.

## 10. Measurement Period

Unless the Licensor publishes a different period, financial thresholds are measured over the immediately preceding twelve months.

If an individual, business, project, or service has existed for less than twelve months, amounts received or earned since inception are used for that initial period.

## 11. Aggregation and Anti-Avoidance

Eligibility is based on the economic reality of the operation.

A Licensee may not avoid thresholds by splitting one business into multiple entities, using affiliates, dividing customers among related deployments, creating multiple accounts, redirecting revenue, misclassifying receipts, or using another arrangement primarily intended to avoid applicable limits.

Entities under common ownership or control may be aggregated for threshold purposes.
